# Project charter

## Purpose

Open Civic exists to make civic participation safer, inspectable, independently deployable, and available without payment. It is a public-good software project, not a business.

## Permanent noncommercial promise

The official project and official services will not:

- charge for access or features;
- display advertising;
- sell, rent, trade, or monetize personal or participation data;
- accept paid placement or paid influence over topics, moderation, eligibility, voting rules, or results;
- create premium participation, moderation, or governance tiers;
- use dark patterns to solicit money or participation.

The AGPL permits independent use of the source under its terms, including uses the official project may not endorse. The charter governs the official project, marks, infrastructure, maintainers, and services; it does not add restrictions to the software license.

## Funding

The project may accept transparent, voluntary support through GitHub Sponsors or grants with no control rights. Reports should disclose the source, amount or range, dates, restricted purpose if any, spending, approvers, and conflicts of interest. Practical redactions may protect donor privacy and account security.

Money may fund infrastructure, messaging, domains, audits, accessibility, translation, incident response, and contributor grants. A financial contribution never buys roadmap priority, moderation power, access to identities, or governance votes.

## Recognition

Financial Sponsors and Country Launch Contributors are separate groups. Volunteer labor is credited through verifiable contributions and is never presented as purchased influence or converted to a fictional monetary value.

## Governance

During the founding phase, decisions are made publicly through issues, pull requests, and architecture decision records. Before a sensitive production launch, governance must include:

- at least three maintainers from more than one organization or household;
- two-person approval for releases, production access, and spending;
- public decision and conflict-of-interest records;
- time-limited and reviewable emergency powers;
- separate operators for services whose collusion would break privacy;
- a documented process to remove a compromised maintainer.

No founder should be technically able to access or change every privacy-critical component alone.

## Changing this charter

Changes require a public proposal, at least 30 days for comment, and approval from at least two-thirds of eligible maintainers. The noncommercial promise and the rule that money buys no influence may only be strengthened, not removed, by the official project.
